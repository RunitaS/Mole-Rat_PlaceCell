# Spike analysis

### Spike sorting
Single units were isolated using semiautomatic approach in spike sorting software SpikieSort3D (Neuralynx, version: 2.5.4).Rough boundaries aund clusters were obtained by applying automatic spike sorting tool Kultakwik to the entire recording file. Boundaries were refined manually to this output and single unit clusters were separated from multi unit clusters for further analysis. 

### Single unit criteria
Single units were clusters were isolated from the recording file as individual .ntt files and screened for signal to noise ratio, ISI violation percentage, and waveform template consistency. 

To calculate Signal to noise ration (SNR) of each single-unit cluster the median absolute deviation was computed for the first three and last three out of the 32 points spanning 5msec of each spike snippet was used to calculate the baseline activity of the unit as: median (|x-meedina(x)|)/0.6745
SNR was calculated as: 20·log10 . A_peak/σ_noise 
where A_peak = max(|mean waveform|)
All units with SNR below 2 were discarded as noisy units.

To compute the inter-spike interval violations, the difference in timestamps between every consecutive pair of spikes was extracted. If more than 1% of total spikes occured within an inter spike interval of 2msec or less, the unit was flagged as multi-unit and not used for further analysis.

To compute the consistency of the waveform of the single-units, the mean waveform was calculated by averaging across waveforms individual 32 point sof the recorded spikes. Pearson correlation scaore was calculated by point to point comparison of individual waveforms with mean waveform. The final averaged correlation value across all four channels of the tetrode was used as the template correlation score. A threhold value r was calculated as:
Σ(amp(x) . amp(i))/ √Σ(amp(x)^2 . Σamp(i)^2)
Units not crossing the threshold value were discarded as noise units and not used for further analysis.

Peak to Trough duration and amplitude ratio was calculated using waveforms on channel with maximum amplitude of spikes using the mean waveform calculated as mentioned above. The peak and trough of the spike were detected as the highest amplitude in positive axis and highest amplitude in negative axis respectively. The peak to trough interval was calculated as the time interval between the two points and the ampltiude ratio was calculated as the amplitude of peak/|amplitude of torugh|.

Spike duration was calculated by first computing the baseline firing ampltiude using median absolute deviation procedure as mentioned in the SNR calculation. THe begining and end of the spike were marked by calculating the point of 2 standard deviation from the baseline amplitude from the starting of the spike recording for begining point and end of recording for spike end point. the average time difference between these two points was defined as the spike duration of that unit.

l-ratio and isolation distance:
The mahalanobis distance was computed by calculating the euclidean distance between mutlivariate data points:
d^2 = (x-μ)^2 . Σ^-1 (x-μ)
This produces an 8 dimension features vector space per spike using energy and peak feature vector space per spike using energy and peak features on 4 channels of the tetrode.

The significance of both isolation distance and l-ratio was statistically tested by performing a spike assignment shuffling procedure 500 times. The cluster qualified as a single unit only if the scores for the original cluster distribution was under 0.05 confidence interval.

### Rate map construction
An occupancy normalized firing rate map was constructed for every single unit that qualified the criteria above. The spike timestamps were matched to their closest tracking timestamps with difference between the two timestamps < 50 msec. The area of the arena was divided into 2 cm x 2 cm bins and tracking data in time omain was tranformed into spatial bins to produce an occupancy map. All bins with occupancy < 1 sec were discarded. Raw spike map was obtained by binning spikes into their corresponding matched position bin. Both raw occupancy and spike map were gaussian smoothed by a σ factor of <TBD>.
Raw rate map was constructed by dividing the raw spike map with the raw occpancy map and the smoothed rate map was obtained by dividing the smoothed spike map by the smoothed occupancy map.

### Place cell criteria

Peak firing rate of the cell was obtained from the bin with the highest firing rate in  the raw rate map.
Spatial inforation score was calculated as: Σpi(ri/r) . log2 (ri/r)
Sparsity score was calculated as: Σ(pi.ri)^2/Σpi.(ri)^2
Coherence score was calculated as the z-transformed correlation value obtained by correlating the firing rate of each bin in smoothed rate map with its 8 Moore's neighbours averaged across all bin. <Padding required at edges!>
z = 0.5 . loge [(1+r)/(1-r)]
Stability score was calculated as the pearson's correlation score between the smoothed rate map of first half and smoothed rate map of second half of the recording session. If a bin was not visited in both sessions for longer than 1 sec, it wasn't included in the calculation of stability score.
A cell qualified as a place cell if the peak firing rate was in the range 1 Hz to 25 Hz, spatial information was above 0.5 bits/spike and passed the shuffling criteria, sparsity score was below 0.5, coherence score passed the shuffling criteria and stability score was above 0.5.

#### Place field criteria
Individual place fields were isolated from rate maps by using a single pass, non-iterative threshold method. Bins were considered as a part of a field within the rate map only if their firing rate was above mean firing rate of the cell and within 20% of peka firing rate. All retained candidate place field bins were grouped using a diagonal inclusive, 8 connected components floodfill method and each isolated field was retained only if it has a minimum of 9 contiguous bins. Boudaries for this bin, total number of fields per cell and area of each field were extracted.

### Speed modulation of firing rate
The modulation of place cells' firing rate with respect to animals' running speed was analyzed both averaged rates across speed bins and instantaneous firing rate. Instantaneous firing rate of a place cell was calculated by matching the spike data time series with the posititon tracking time series and calculating the number of spikes occuring within the tracing frame to frame interval. All instantaneous bins with zero spike were removed from the ananlysis for averaged speed modulation but retained for instantaneous speed modulation analysis. Instantaneous firing trace was gaussian smoothed by a factor σ = 1 for instantaneous speed modulation analysis.
To avoid spurious correlations from non-specific firng of place cells during periods of immobility, epochs with speed values below 1cm/sec were discarded in both analyses.
Averaged speed modulation of place cells: All non-zero retained epochs of instantaneous speed and their corresponding firing rate values were binned in 4cm/sec wide intervals spanning the range 1 to 80cm/sec. The median values of the it's member samples within the bin was considered as the representative value for that bin to reduce the influence of outlier high firing bursts.
Speed bins with less than 0.2% of all retianed samples were discarded to avoid bias due to sparsely sampled bins. A weighted linear regression of binned median rate against bin centers was calculated to avoid bias due to uneven sampling. The speeed score was calculated as wighted pearson correlation coefficent from this fit. 
Instantaneous speed-firing rate modulation of place cells: Speed score was calculated as sample by sample pearson correlation between smooth speed and smoothed firing rate traces across time. 
The significance of both averaged and instantaneous speed modulation of firing rate of place cells was assesed by shuffling the spike trains similar to procedure in spatial information significance testing and calculating the speed scores for 1000 shuffled spike trains. A cell was classifed as significantly speed modulated cell only if the speed score for original spike train were either below 2.5% or above 97.5% percentile rage for both averaged and speed shuffling analysis to account for both positive (p-speed cell) and negative (n-speed cell) speed modulation of place cell firing (Iwase et.al. 2020).

## PSD plot using FOOOF
Due to mechanical artifacts introduced in our LFP recordings below 2Hz range, we cleaned the LFP signal by calculating the power of the signal in noise range (1-2 Hz) and tentative theta frequency window range (2-14Hz) and rejecting epochs where power in noise range exceeded power of the signal in target frequency range. Addittionaly, a median absolute deviation detection was run on epochs retained after noise rejection filtering which removed outliers with z > 5. Power spectral density (PSD) was computed using all retained epochs post filtering using Welch's method with a non-overlapping Hann window. To remove any residual line noise traces from the signal, spectral range at 50 Hz and its harmonic components were removed from the PSD plot and interpolated in +-2Hz windows centered at each harmonic frequency. 
To make the frequency spectrum between differnet animals comparable, independent of absolute signal amplitude, the PSD was normalized by relative power by dividing by its own total power in 1 to 100 Hz range. 
Fitting Oscillations and One Over F (FOOOF) model was used to estimate the aperiodc signal which enabled running the characterization of oscillation in hippocampus purely on the period component of the signal.
Normalized PSD obtained from each individual recording files was fit with FOOOF model in the 1-40 Hz frequency range using the knee mode which incorporates the sharp bend in neural PSD usually occuring at lower frequencies in the range 1-10 Hz of neural signals. Peaks were detected using a peak width limit of 1-8 Hz width, with maximum limit of 6 peak per power spectrum and minimum relative height of 0.1 with a peak threshold of 2 standard deviation above power of the aperiodic fit. 
The fit quality of FOOOF was assessed by computing the r^2 value and model error and rejecting recording with r^2 values below 0.9 and model error value of 0.4. We calculated the peak frequency and width of the peaks (using Full Width Half Maximum method) which were used to characterize the dominant frequency and it's range in mole-rat hippocampus.
PSD plots for recordings across same animal were pooled and plotted as mean power +- SEM (in relative power units).

## Autocorrelogram analysis for theta epoch and theta continuity estimation
The autocorrelogram method used in Dunn et.al. 2022 was used to detect significant theta epochs in the theta range quantified from FOOOF analysis mentioned above. Raw LFP signal was downsampled from 32 kHz to 1 kHz and preprocessed by applying zero phase IIR notch filter at 50 Hz and its harmonics with quality factor 30. The signal was linearly detrended and a zero phase 4th order Butterworth low-pass filter was applied with a 100 Hz cutoff.
Similar to LFP cleaning procedure used in FOOOF analysis, 1 sec epochs with power in slow drift artifact range (1-3 Hz) exceeding power in theta frequenct range (3-7 Hz) were rejected. Peak to peak amplitude outliers exceeding z value of 5 were also rejected. Clean epochs retained post filtering steps were used for further ACG analysis.
Movment classification: Continuously tracked animal position corrected for jumps and Gaussian smoothed with sigma 1 were used to calculate speed values using frame to frame displacement. LFP data was time matched to tracking data by finding the nearest timestamp range and assigning the median speed value for the epoch.
Sinuoid reference bank quantification: A reference bank of autocorelograms consisting of pure sinusoids in range 3-7 Hz at 0.1 Hz interva (41 reference sinusoids) were created to correlate with epochs of real LFP signal.
The real LFP signal was chunked into 1 sec nonoverlapping bins. An ACG was computed and normalized so its zero lag value equalled 1 for each 1 sec epoch which was correlated with all reference sinusoids. The euclidean distance between each correlated signal was estimated by calculating point to point squared difference. The sine yielding the lowest Euclidean distance (ED_min) was used as the matched theta frequency provided the euclidean distance was below 1. The peak range of the epoch was calculated by dividing the peak height of first local maxima post zero lag peak minus mean of it neighing troughs by the first peak if the matched reference sinusoid and it's repective troughs. 
Peak range = data epoch AC range/matched sinusoid AC range
Non-theta epoch rejection: Euclidean distance values above 1 indicate a complete phase mistmatch implying that no sinusoid from the reference bank matched the LFP epoch. All epochs wit ED_min > 1 were rejected and flagged as non-theta epochs. 
Epochs retained after this filter were grouped into one of two movement classes. Epochs with median speed value > 4cm/sec were grouped into mobility epoch class and epochs with median speed value < 4 cm/sec. The theta characteristics including centroids of their distribution atocorrelation vs frequency range distribution, the autocorrelation range, and their frequency ranges were staitstically analyzed for differences between the two classes. 

## Theta depth profile:
Theta characteristics across layers of the hippocampus was computed from neuropixels 2.0 LFP recordings.
Preprocessing: Broadband neural signal was loaded using spikeinterface and preprocessed by performing phase shift correction for interchannel samoling delays, automatic detection of bad channels, bandpass filtering using 4th order Butterworth filter in 1 to 500 Hz range and applying common median global referencing correction across the probe for common mode noise rejection.
The cleaned data was downsampled from 30 KHz to 1 kHz and motion correcion was applied using DREDge which estimates slow drift in signal using a frame to frame correlation matric and feeding it into a Bayesian moedl with spatiotemporal smoothing prior. All surviving clean channels mapped to hippocampal subregions were arranged serially in the cortical to subcortical  axis (dorsoventrla brain axis) to obtain top to bottom depth sequence of LFP signal.
Noisy epoch rejection: THe entire signal was scanned in 1 sec non-pverlapping bins and all epochs with power in slow drift artifact range (1-3 Hz) exceeding power in theta frequency range (3-7 Hz) and exceeded the MD threshold of z >5, the epoch was flagged as noise and rejected from further analysis.
Theta power depth profile: Mean theta band power integrated across range 3-7 Hz was averaged across all clean epochs retained post denoising of signal for all hippocampal channels individually.
Theta phase depth profile: All theta phase values were calculated using theta phase on top most channel as reference theta signal. The first theta phase on reference channel was phase value of 0°. The time lag between 0° phase timepoint and theta peak occuring post this timepoint was used to calculate phase offset as: 2π . |lag|T/T  where T=reference channel's own median interpeak interval. 
Peaks had to cross a prominence threshold value of 0.5 trace standard deviation. 

## Spike theta coupling
To screen place cells for theta modulatio the preprocessed and cleaned LFP signal was bandpassed in the theta range (3-7 Hz) using a zero pass 4th order Butterworth filter.

Phase estimation of theta: Instantaneous theta phase was estimated using Generalized Phase Approach (adapted from Davis Muller et.al. 2020) instead of the standard Hilbert method. Briefly, the analytic signal was computed using the Hilbert transformand the instantaneous phase values were computed form this analytic signal. All out-of-theta-bound phase values were extracted as they signify unreliable potentially phase reversed segments as theta oscillations isn't perfectly sinusoidal. These epochs were extended in width by factor of 3 and their phase values were reconstructed by shape-preserving interpolation (PCHIP) of te unwrapped phase trend from neighbouring reliable values.
Spike phase estimation: Both sine and cosine values of unwrapped phase of theta signal were interpolated separately to avid errors of 0° to 360° wrap around. Each spike wastime matched to LFP time series and assigned a theta phase obtained from the Generalized Phase Approach. To vizualize the theta phase preference of spike firing, spikes were binned in 36° wide phase itnervals ad the spikes occuring were assigned their respective bins which was plotted as a polar histogram and checked for significance by calculating the mean resultant vector length (MRL) and performing Rayleigh test for circular non-uniformity with 0.05 confidence interval. 
Theta Modulation of place cells: The strength og theta modulation of place cells was assessed by calculating the THeta Modulation Index (TMI) (adapted from Frank et.al. 2001). The spike phase were tiled across 5 repeated 360° cycles (0° to 1800°) and Gaussian smoothed. Middle two tiles (0° to 720°) with 36° wide bins were normalized with respect to the maximum spike bin. The TMI was calculated as 1 minus spikes in minimum bin. Significance of theta modulation wass assessed by performing shuffling analysis wherein the original spike train was shuffled by an interval of atleast 20 msec in groups of 50 msec to preserve the bursting dynamic of the cell. This procedure was repeated 1000 times and the TMI was calculated for each surrogate spike train. The place cell was considered as significantly theta modulated only if the TMI score of its original spike train exceeded the 95 percentile range compared to the TMI scores of the shuffled spiek trains.
Theta phase recession analysis: Phase precession analysis requires the position data to e a 1 dimensional array. However, since we recorded place cells in both 1D and 2D arena, we adapted the pass index method used by Climer et.a;. 2013. The pass index method transforms the spiking activity with respect to 1D passes through the field enabling phase precession analysis to be performed on 2D data. 
Pass index analysis: A 2D occupancy normalized firng rate map was computed with 2 x 2 cm spatial bins and Gaussian smoohed by smoothing factor ___. Place fields were isolated by extracting all bin with firing rate above cell's mean firign rate and within 20% of cell's mean firing rate. Only peak fields with peak firing rate bin and it's more than 9 contiguous connected neighbours were analyzed for phase precession. The rate map was normalized with respect to the peak firing and minimum firing range assigning each bin a field index value in range 1 to 0. Every tracked position of the animal was assigned its respective field index value and this trajectory was rearranged in equal intervals of distance travelled. THe tracking signal was denoised and Hilbert transformed to extract the pass index value across the field with -1 indicating entry into the field, 0  at the field center, and +1 indicating exit position through the field. 
Circular linear regression analysis: Each spike's linear pass index and theta phase extracted using the generalized phase approach were correlated using a circular-linear correlation analysis (adapted from Kempter et.al. 2012). Briefly the slope and correlation coeeficient values were calculated by maximinzing the resultant vector R of regression fit. Signficance was assessed bycorrelation coefficent, number of spikes in the field, pass index dispersion, by performing a asymptotic z-rest. 
We analyzed place cells for both phase precession and procession. Significant negative sloped cells were classified as phase precessing place cells and cells with positive slopes were classified as phase processing. The spike theta phase relationship characteristics like correlation coefficient score, sloe, and theta range were statistically compared across 3 different arenas ison Kruskal Wallis test.
