MARGIN_FRAMES = int(fps)   # 1 second of frames at either end
The reference (Fenton) code excludes shifts within 20 seconds of a full wrap (start_t = 20000000 µs, end_t = duration - 20000000 µs). The current code only excludes shifts within 1 second (int(fps) = 30 frames at 30 fps).

Why this matters: a circular time-shift null model only works if the shift is large enough to fully decorrelate the spike train's short-timescale structure (bursts, theta-cycle spiking, repeated visits to the same location) from the position track. A 1-second exclusion zone is far too narrow — bursty or theta-modulated cells can retain spurious spatial coherence for shifts of several seconds. This inflates the upper tail of the null SIR distribution, which is exactly what determines the 95th-percentile pass/fail threshold in _run_bootstrap. A real place cell with genuinely high SIR can fail to clear an artificially inflated bootstrap_p95 — precisely the "high SIR, low sparsity, fails bootstrap" pattern you're seeing.

For a session of, say, 20 minutes at 30 fps (~36,000 frames), only ~0.17% of the shift range is excluded at each edge with the current margin — versus a much larger, deliberately conservative exclusion zone in the reference. Even a small fraction of "under-shifted" draws among 1000 bootstrap iterations can land in the top 5% and drag the p95 threshold up.

Fix: raise MARGIN_FRAMES to match the reference's intent, e.g. MARGIN_FRAMES = 20 * fps (20 s), or make it a named constant like MIN_SHIFT_S = 20.
